\section{Security Identity and Temporal Coverage Verification}

\textbf{Observed.} Every successful Yahoo checkpoint was compared with the
security master using the security identifier, reference-date ticker, Yahoo
ticker, provider, and date key. Of \YahooSuccess{} successful histories,
\IdentityValid{} passed all identity and schema tests; \IdentityInvalid{} were
quarantined. No duplicate checkpoint dates or exact cross-security
price--volume histories were found. \PlaceholderCheckpoints{} checkpoints
contained date grids with all-NaN price placeholders; their \PlaceholderRows{}
placeholder rows remain in the raw audit archive and are excluded from the
research base.

\textbf{Observed.} The apparent equality between \YahooSuccess{} successful
histories and \YahooSuccess{} securities observed in 2025 is not a
cumulative-count artifact. Every
successful security has at least one positive-volume row in 2025. The temporal

\textbf{Conclusion and uncertainty.} This supports checkpoint attribution and
rejects a simple cumulative-count explanation for 2025. It does not prove
continuous trading: \StoppedTradingCandidates{} securities have at least ten
trailing sessions after their last positive-volume trade with unchanged
zero-volume rows. They are flagged as stopped-trading candidates because
verified delisting dates remain unavailable. An identity-valid checkpoint is
therefore a prerequisite for descriptive use, not proof of an uninterrupted
listing.

\textbf{Recommended action.} Preserve the checkpoint identity audit, exclude
all-NaN placeholders from analytical bases, and review trailing zero-volume
tails before time-series feature construction.

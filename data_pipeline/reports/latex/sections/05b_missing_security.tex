usable history for \YahooSuccess{} and no usable history for
\UnavailableSecurities{}, an unavailable share of \UnavailableShare{}. A Yahoo
miss is not treated as a delisting determination.

concentrated among securities without a current exact SEC ticker and those with
SEC-evidenced ticker changes. These are rates within reported metadata cohorts,
not estimates for the unknown complete listed population.

\textbf{Targeted recovery.} The SEC evidence identifies
\SafeAliasCandidates{} securities with a single later-symbol candidate,
\AmbiguousAliasCandidates{} with multiple or ambiguous candidates, and
\MissingNoAlias{} with no later-symbol evidence. A bounded probe of
\ProbeAttempts{} unambiguous aliases found vendor data for \ProbeFound{}, but
none were admitted to the primary dataset because security continuity across
the alias transition must first be established.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Missingness can materially alter later samples of renamed,
delisted, merged, or security-class-heavy issuers. The acquired Yahoo panel must
not be described as representative. Targeted recovery is feasible, but a broad
automatic alias substitution would risk assigning unrelated or preferred
security histories.
